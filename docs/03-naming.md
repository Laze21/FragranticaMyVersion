# Naming sprint

The product runs under a replaceable token (`APP_NAME` in `src/lib/config.ts`, overridable with
`NEXT_PUBLIC_APP_NAME`). Nothing in the architecture, the database or the URLs depends on the name.
Conflict screens are in `docs/research/naming-and-ai-tells.md`; this is the shortlist and the reasoning.

## What the name has to do

Memorable, pronounceable, short, distinctive, usable internationally, able to become a noun ("put it on my Wake"),
not fake-luxury, not startup-generic, not a literal description. It should sit beside Fragrantica, Parfumo and
Basenotes without sounding like a response to them.

## Territories explored (34 serious candidates)

| Territory | Candidates |
|---|---|
| Scent vocabulary | Sillage, Drydown, Accord, Accordant, Flacon, Mouillette, Blotter, Decant, Vial, Atomizer, Spritz, Tester, Mouillé |
| Air and trace | Waft, Whiff, Plume, Linger, Trace, Wake, Volatile, Strata |
| Objects and ritual | Pomander, Alembic, Tincture, Snifter, Nosegay, Counter, Shelfie, Rotation, Nosebook |
| Collecting and culture | Facet, Ambit, Heartwood, Wristful, Nosework, Bottlenose, Pulsepoint, Smellbound |

## Finalists

| Name | Why it works | Downside | Pronunciation | Personality | URL ideas |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Wake** | Sillage literally means a ship's wake: the trail a scent leaves. One syllable, a real word people already use, and it becomes a noun and a verb without effort ("on my Wake", "wake it"). It also names the product's signature visual, the Trail. No fragrance business found under exactly this name. | A very common word: weak as a trademark and hard to own in search; "wake" (funeral) is a second meaning. | /weɪk/ | Calm, observant, slightly dry. | wake.app, wakenotes.com, getwake.com, wake.fragrance |
| **Bottlenose** | A pun that explains itself (bottles + nose), friendly, impossible to confuse with luxury puffery, instantly a mascot and a noun. Only a defunct analytics company found. | Long; the dolphin association could read as cute rather than cultured. | /ˈbɒtəlnəʊz/ | Playful, knowledgeable, a little nerdy. | bottlenose.app, bottlenose.co |
| **Blotter** | The paper strip every counter hands you: the ritual of trying before buying. Short, tactile, and the strip is already a visual device in the interface. No fragrance conflict found. | Police-blotter and LSD connotations in the US. | /ˈblɒtə/ | Editorial, hands-on. | blotter.app, blotterclub.com |
| **Wristful** | A coined word with a clear image (a wrist full of scent) and no conflicts found anywhere. | Coined words take longer to feel established; slightly twee. | /ˈrɪstfʊl/ | Warm, personal. | wristful.com (likely available), wristful.app |
| **Volatile** | The chemistry of scent itself (volatile compounds) and the mood of a community that argues about reformulations. Distinctive, cultured. | "Unstable" is the everyday meaning; one perfume named "Volatile Woman". | /ˈvɒlətaɪl/ | Sharp, opinionated. | volatile.fm, volatilenotes.com |
| **Accordant** | From "accord", the vocabulary of blending; sounds settled and adult. | Stiff; an ERP reseller and a care programme use it. | /əˈkɔːdənt/ | Measured, archival. | accordant.app |

## Decision for the prototype

**Wake** is the working name: it is the only finalist that names the product's own idea (the trail a scent leaves)
and reads naturally in sentences. It is carried as a token so a final decision, after proper trademark clearance and
handle checks, costs nothing to apply. Bottlenose is the recommended fallback if the common-word weakness of Wake
proves decisive.

This is preliminary desk research, not legal clearance.
